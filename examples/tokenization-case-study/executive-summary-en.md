# Executive Summary: EIB Digital Bond and Conditions for Korean Adoption

> Analysis date: 16 November 2026 · Not legal or investment advice

The European Investment Bank's £50 million sterling digital bond used a private blockchain as the record of legal ownership and a public blockchain mirror for anonymised transparency. HSBC acted as the central account keeper on Orion, while established institutions provided custody. [EV-EIB-GBP-2023]

The transaction should not be described as an EU DLT Pilot issuance. It was announced on 31 January 2023, before Regulation (EU) 2022/858 began to apply on 23 March 2023. [EV-EIB-GBP-2023][EV-EU-DLTR-2022]

Korea's amended Electronic Securities Act was promulgated on 3 February 2026 but will not take effect until 4 February 2027. The Financial Services Commission's policy roadmap prioritises privately placed bonds and MMFs for institutional investors in the first stage. Proposed subordinate rules address ledger participation, issuer account managers and OTC venues, but they remained draft rules at the analysis date. [EV-KR-ESA-21318][EV-KR-FSC-POLICY-20260904][EV-KR-FSC-DRAFT-20261001]

The closest initial Korean model is therefore an institutional private bond on a permissioned ledger connected to the Korea Securities Depository and licensed account-management, intermediation and custody functions. A public mirror should be treated as an optional transparency layer, not the legal record. Cash settlement should initially remain linked to established payment rails; on-chain settlement requires separate legal and infrastructure readiness.

Commercial opportunities may arise in issuance, account management, custody, reconciliation, lifecycle automation and compliance tooling. These are supported inferences, not proven revenue forecasts. Final subordinate legislation, licensing scope, KSD technical standards, privacy controls, settlement finality and operational liability must be verified before implementation.
